(From [Fischer and Arendt, 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1002/jez.b.22486))
>"As characteristic for spiral cleavage, the bulk of mesoderm forms from one cell, the 4d blastomere. This process has not yet been followed in cellular detail in annelids except in the leech, where mesoteloblasts, a pair of mesodermal stem cells, generate two bands of mesodermprecursor cells in an iterative fashion. It is so far unknown whether such stem cell‐like lineage is a general property of 4d‐derived mesoderm in spiralian larvae. To address this, we have analyzed the cell lineage of the 4d blastomere in the polychaete annelid Platynereis dumerilii, an emerging model for lophotrochozoan and spiralian embryology (Fischer et al., 2010), by 4D microscopy, a semi‐automated cell tracking technique based on differential interference contrast serial imaging"



Fischer and Arendt, 2013 explore the role of the 4d- blastomere in errant polychaetes such as *Platynereis dumerilii*.  This was done by following 4D recordings in 5 individuals, where each individual was tracked until the beginning of the early trochophore stage (close to 24 hpf, when the larva become free floating). The authors found that the first cell division of the 4d blastomere (which they refer to as the M-blastomere in their work) is equal and gives rise to Ml and Mr daughter cells which are equal in size. These cells produce the left and right mesoderm bands respectively. Subsequently, these daughter cells undergo 6 unequal, consecutive divisions. During this period, the smaller daughter cells remain quiescent. Once the larger daughter cell of the sixth cycle divides equally, then soon after, the previously quiescent daughter cells start proliferating and expanding the mesodermal band.
