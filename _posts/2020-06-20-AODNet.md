(From  [Li et al, 2017](AOD-Net: All-in-One Dehazing Network))
>"All above methods share the same belief, that in order to recover a clean scene from haze, it is key to estimate an accurate medium transmission map.  The atmospheric light is calculated separately, and the clean image is recovered based on the atmospheric scattering model. Albeit being intuitive and physically grounded, such a procedure does not directly measure or minimize the reconstruction distortions. As a result, it will undoubtedly give rise to the sub-optimal image restoration quality."

Li and colleagues state that contemporarry deep learning methods for dehazing estimate the coefficients of the atmosphere scattering model independently which leads to inferior results. Hence, they propose a reformulation of the atmosphere scattering model. The atmospheric scattering model for hazy image generation is as follows:

$$  I(x) = J(x) t(x) + A (1 - t(x)) $$

$$ I(x) = J(x) e^{-\beta d(x)} + A \left( 1 - e^{-\beta d(x)} \right) $$

Here, I(x) is the hazy image, J(x) is the clean, unknown image, and $\beta$ is the unknown, scattering coefficient of the atmosphere, $A$ is the unknown, background atmospheric light and d(x) is the distance away from the sample. 

I wondered whether this model could be used to dehaze images acquired through light-sheet imaging. In that scenario, perhaps $A$ doesn't need to be estimated (? Not sure), because it can be evaluated by averaging the intensity in the slices far-away from the sample. I also wonder if this model actually holds for light-sheet imaging. Perhaps the second term indicating the contribution of the atmosphere is not needed at all.

Anyway, the authors suggest that the equation above can be re-written as follows:

$$ J(x) = \frac{1}{t(x)} I(x) - A \frac{1}{t(x)} + A  $$
which is equivalent to stating that:

$$ J(x) = \frac{I(x) - A}{t(x)} + A $$

or

$$ J(x) = I(x) K (x) - K(x) + b $$ 

where 

$$ K(x) = \frac{\frac{I(x)-A}{t(x)} + \left(A -b \right) }{I(x) - 1} $$.  

Note that this formulation includes I(x) in the definition of K(x). The advantage of this is that K(x) is now a context-dependent function of I(x) with additional joint, dependency on $\beta$ and A. It is not clear to me why the authors introduced the bias term $b$. Eliminating it from the equation should give equivalent results, I think currently.

With this formulation in tow, one needs to estimate K(x) which varies for each image I(x) and jointly captures the dependency on A and $\beta$. This leads to an end-to-end dehazing result. For the training, the authors synthetical hazed some images using different $\beta$ and $A$. I wonder if it would work to synthetically haze confocal images and then use the trained network to instead dehaze light-sheet data. 
 The authors showed best results on a test set (which they refer to as TestSet B in the publication) which is quite different from the training image set. Additionally they show that the dehazing module incorporates well with higher level tasks such as object detection.
