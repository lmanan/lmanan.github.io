[Keuper et al, 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.06973.pdf) introduced the idea of Lifted Multicuts to tackle some of the challenges faced by Multicuts. Firstly, the authors introduce the advantages of the minimum cost multicut (MP) framework for the purpose of image decomposition. They state that these are easy and intuitive to use - all you need is a pixel grid and an edge probability score (say, generated by a CNN) and one ends up with an 0-1 output which indicates whether the edge should be joined (0) or cut (1). However, MP is also known to be NP-hard, so running it on the complete image is impractical. Hence, what [other authors](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth.4151?proof=t%25C2%25A0)  often do is to first group pixels into super pixels, say using a variant of the seeded watershed approach (where the seeds are considered to be the local maxima from some predicted probability map), and then running the MP essentially to reduce the number of candidates. A second disadvantage is that the MP approach only makes explicit for edges whether the incident nodes are in distinct components. It does not make explicit for pairs of nodes that are not neighbors whether these are in distinct components. This is corrected for in the contribution of Keuper et al, 2015 which they refer to as the Lifted Multicut Problem (LMP).
