(From [Clark et al, 2022](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.01.26.477848v1.full.pdf))
>"And yet, there is also wide variation across species in the timing of segmentation and the geometry of the embryonic fate map. The majority of described species exhibit sequential segmentation during posterior growth. Other
species, such as the fly Drosophila melanogaster, specify almost all of their segments simultaneously prior to gastrulation. The dynamics of both sequential segmentation and simultaneous segmentation correlate with the spatiotemporal expression of the “timer” genes caudal, Dichaete and odd-paired, which are expressed sequentially within segmenting tissues."

[Clark et al, 2022](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.01.26.477848v1.full.pdf) in their recent preprint explore how morphological segmentation in Drosophila is coordinated by dynamic gene expression. Their exploration stems from the quandary that in many organisms, morphological segmentation along the anterior-posterior axis happens sequentially, while in Drosophila, it is a simultaneous process. The authors argue that understanding how gene expression responsible for segmentation is coordinated over time and across the embryo is a question of developmental and evolutionary importance. In previous works by the authors, they have identified a framework of `timer genes' which broadly regulates expression in time and space. But the motivation  for this work stems from the question regarding what spatiotemporally regulates these timer genes themselves!

What attracted me to this paper, apart from the clear elucidation of the problem statement and terminology, is the formulation of the regulatory network as a logical computational model.
