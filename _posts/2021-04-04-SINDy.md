(From [Brunton et al, 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1517384113))
>"Data is abundant while models remain elusive, as in climate science, neuroscience, ecology, finance, and epidemiology, to name only a few examples."


Brunton et al, 2016 propose *SINDy* (Sparse Identification of Non-Linear Dynamics) - a way to discover the underlying physical model at play. They base the motivation of the work to build models which are generalizable and interpretable (something which deep learning models are frequently criticized for - *generalization* on out of distribution data is a big area of research and DNNs are notorious for the *lack of interpretability* to explain the values of the different, learnt  weights). Additionally, the authors put an emphasis on *sparsity* - they state that most physical models depend on only a few constitutive terms.

>"The key observation is that for many systems of interest, the function 𝐟 consists of only a few terms, making it sparse in the space of possible functions. "

In addition, the authors' approach is able to handle noisy measurement data and can deal with both ordinary and partial differential equations. 

I find it surprising that despite the wealth of volumetric microscopy data which is typically generated in microscopy imaging in developmental biology labs, identifying the nature of the model through this approach is absent to my knowledge.
