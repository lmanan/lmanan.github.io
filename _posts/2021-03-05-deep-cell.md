(From [Moen et al, 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/803205v2.full.pdf)) 
>"Increasingly, image analysis is a bottleneck for discovery as there is a gap between our ability to collect and analyze data. This gap can be traced to the limited accuracy and generality of cell segmentation and tracking algorithms. These limitations lead to a significant curation time, recently estimated to be > 100 hours for one manuscript of data"

Moen et al provide 4 annotated datasets:
- `hek293` (1.3 GiB) : 259 sequences of shape 30, 135, 160 (T, Y, X)
- `hela-s3` (6.4 GiB): 180 sequences of shape 40, 216, 256 (T, Y, X)
- `nih_3t3` (3.2 GiB): 240 sequences of shape 30, 154, 182 (T, Y, X)
- `raw2647` (2.2 GiB): 124 sequences of shape 30, 202, 240 (T, Y, X)


Moen et al state that engineering the cost function to associate detections leads to suboptimal results. Hence, they propose to learn this cost function. To construct this learnt cost function, they consider it as a classification task. For each pair of detected cells arising from two frames, `deepcell` learns the probability of whether the two cells are the same, or different or share a parent-child relationship. Then the final tracks are determined by looking at a neighbourhood of 7 frames.
